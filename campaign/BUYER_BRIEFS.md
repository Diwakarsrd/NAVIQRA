# Strategic buyer briefs

These are hypotheses for tailored conversations, not claims of buyer interest or confirmed product gaps. Validate each with the recipient.

## Airbnb — primary strategic target
**Potential fit:** cross-provider trip context, portable traveler preferences, group decision workflows, and disruption orchestration that can complement a marketplace and its own AI travel experiences.  
**NAVIQRA wedge:** provider-neutral orchestration and portable memory; Airbnb retains its marketplace, identity, payments, supply, and booking authority.  
**Suggested ask:** 30-minute architecture review with travel product/AI infrastructure leadership; evaluate one bounded use case such as group consensus or disruption recovery.  
**Do not say:** “We replace Airbnb’s travel agent” or imply Airbnb needs to acquire NAVIQRA.

## Expedia Group
**Potential fit:** multi-brand travel distribution, itinerary context, and cross-supplier servicing.  
**Wedge:** reusable orchestration and recovery layer across supplier adapters.  
**Ask:** technical evaluation of provider abstraction and approval-gated recovery.

## Booking Holdings
**Potential fit:** broad accommodation supply and AI-assisted trip discovery/service.  
**Wedge:** portable memory and group intent that can enrich discovery without taking over the marketplace.  
**Ask:** evaluate adapter contract and memory portability in a sandbox.

## Amadeus
**Potential fit:** travel technology infrastructure and enterprise distribution ecosystem.  
**Wedge:** open-source agent orchestration primitives and auditable integration boundary.  
**Ask:** architecture feedback and potential partner integration discussion.

## Sabre
**Potential fit:** travel retailing, distribution, and servicing workflows.  
**Wedge:** agent runtime and human-approved recovery workflow around existing distribution systems.  
**Ask:** technical review of adapter boundary and operational controls.

## Buyer qualification questions
1. Which travel workflow is strategically important but costly to integrate across suppliers?
2. What existing system must remain authoritative for pricing, availability, identity, and booking?
3. What security, privacy, and latency requirements would block a pilot?
4. What measurable result would justify a 30-day technical evaluation?
5. Who owns the integration decision and what is the approval path?
